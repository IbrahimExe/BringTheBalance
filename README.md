# Bring The Balance
Teaching gamers & museum-goers about the intricacies of crafting difficulty balance from a developer's perspective. A project created for The Canadian Video Game Museum.

Working with a 3-month time limit, my team of 6 was tasked with creating an exhibit for the Canadian Video Game Museum that teaches the public about the difficulties developers face when trying to balance difficulty in their games. 

Our client, Ryan Dunnison with the Canadian Video Game Museum, was an experienced developer and avid gamer, so our project would have to work for everyone and live up to massive expectations. 

Each of us set learning goals at the start of the project so that we could come out of this with a new set of skills. For myself, I wanted to gain a better familiarity with one of the fastest opted game engines out there; Unreal Engine, and the creation of a game foundation that uses both Blueprints AND Object Oriented C++ programming in engine.

#### LEVEL 1 - Hello World

Initially as a developer coming into this project, I was really astounded by how much input I was able to provide in our initial ideation phase. Our first task was to research the intricacies of games that spanned several genres (i.e. MOBAs, Shooters, Turn-Based RPGs), as well as specific games, such as Overwatch, Pokemon and even whacky, yet very insightful, games such as [Totally Accurate Battle Simulator](https://store.steampowered.com/app/508440/Totally_Accurate_Battle_Simulator/) in order to really nail down the essence of game design, and what made a game *feel* fair, instead of frustrating.

We viewed several lens of how players often complained about how *bad* balance is really easy to criticize and even feel, often resulting in a player-base taking online to complain about how a character is "Too O.P (over powered)", or how this system needs a "rework", when several game designers and developers have tried to draw up how it would look in an often vast scenario with plenty of other factors, such as other characters and a plethora of other game mechanics. That being said, *good* game balance is often always never appreciated enough, and thats the appreciation and learning we landed on making the players feel and understand.

Through our research, we found that 'healthy' game balance is often an intricate and delicate mix of math AND psychology. Games rely on slight, deliberate power asymmetries and character archetypes rather than identical matchups to maintain meaningful strategic choices, rigorous, data-driven play testing is required to continue make the game not only feel balanced, but also KEEP IT FUN! 

<img width="750" height="1000" alt="breif-breakdown" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/85fe4818-3146-4b00-821f-a94edb4b233a" />

We realized that, while 1-1 symmetry is literally the definition of balanced, same heroes, same attack power, same map, same everything- this often led to a very boring game, so PERFECT symmetry was not the right answer either!

#### LEVEL 2 - Are we the game designers or YOU?

Through our first, and weekly ongoing meetings with our client; [The Canadian Video Game Museum](https://cvgm.ca/home.html) team & [Ryan Dunnison](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryandunnison/), we tried to really nail down what kind of experience we wanted to create and provide to people visiting this museum dedicated to the history and celebration of game development here in Canada and Vancouver!

From the creation of personas for the potential visitors and players of our game, to then us having to individually pitch our own ideas, each with a core game loop and basic mechanics and gameplay, we covered almost every genre it felt like!
During the first sprint, where overall genre and gameplay wasn't yet nailed down, I pitched the following games:

i) A one lane, 1v1 MOBA mini-game with character classes and the ability for the player to pick 'Buffs' and 'Nerfs' in what would be a rouge-like mechanic:
<img width="3867" height="6165" alt="Initial Ideation_1" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/c02d6ed5-9c33-40a1-af39-1282e12a6634" />

ii) Simulation game where player feedback is given through the game's 'Steam Page Reviews'
<img width="4110" height="6165" alt="Initial Ideation_2" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/3cf81d7b-0e07-4a58-a266-7d75feb6bef4" />

After initial feedback from the CVGM team and lots of introspection amongst ourselves, and the use of a decision matrix to decide our genre, we landed on a *Turn-Based RPG*, and went right back to the drawing board. 

Here are the ideas I had for our second sprint having our genre nailed down!
<img width="7947" height="6165" alt="Projects III Team 4 - Sprint 2 - Ibrahim PVP Genre Ideation" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/974deb43-ab06-43d4-8a84-c64584a5b51c" />

After even more feedback and lots of discussions within our little team too, we also nailed down that a turn-based, simulated RPG inside a *meta-game setting* would work the best inside the museum as it allows for a 3-5 minute window we established that we wanted the entire experience to have.

The player would not control the characters' actions directly, but they would adjust their stats and run a game simulation to see the results of the changes they made!
